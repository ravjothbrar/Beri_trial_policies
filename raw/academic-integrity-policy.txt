ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICY
Haberdashers' Elstree Schools

Introduction

Academic integrity is fundamental to learning and scholarship at Haberdashers' Elstree Schools. This policy outlines the school's expectations regarding honest academic work and the consequences of academic misconduct.

All members of the school community - students, staff, and parents - share responsibility for maintaining high standards of academic integrity.

What is Academic Integrity?

Academic integrity means:

• Submitting work that is your own
• Properly acknowledging the work and ideas of others
• Following examination and assessment rules
• Being honest about your academic abilities and achievements
• Respecting intellectual property rights
• Conducting research ethically

Academic integrity requires students to:

• Complete their own work to the best of their ability
• Cite sources correctly
• Seek help appropriately
• Follow all assessment guidelines
• Be honest in all academic matters

What is Academic Misconduct?

Academic misconduct includes any action that gives a student an unfair academic advantage or undermines the integrity of assessment. This includes:

Plagiarism

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's work or ideas as your own. This includes:

• Copying text from books, articles, websites, or other sources without citation
• Paraphrasing someone else's work without proper acknowledgment
• Submitting work written by someone else (including parents, tutors, or online services)
• Using AI-generated content without acknowledgment (see Appendix A)
• Copying another student's work
• Resubmitting your own previous work without permission (self-plagiarism)

Even if plagiarism is unintentional, it is still considered academic misconduct. Students are expected to learn and follow proper citation practices.

Cheating

Cheating includes:

• Using unauthorised materials or assistance during examinations or assessments
• Looking at another student's work during a test
• Using mobile phones or other electronic devices during examinations
• Communicating with other students during examinations
• Taking unauthorised notes into an examination
• Copying homework or coursework from other students

Fabrication

Fabrication includes:

• Making up data, sources, or quotations
• Falsifying research results
• Inventing references or citations

Collusion

Collusion is unauthorised collaboration with others. This includes:

• Working with others on an assignment that should be completed individually
• Allowing another student to copy your work
• Sharing answers or assignment solutions with other students

Other Forms of Academic Misconduct

• Impersonation - having someone else take an exam or complete an assignment on your behalf
• Unauthorised access to examination papers or assignments before they are due
• Tampering with or falsifying academic records

Proper Citation and Referencing

Students are expected to acknowledge all sources used in their work. This includes:

• Books and journal articles
• Websites and online resources
• Videos and podcasts
• Interviews and personal communications
• AI tools (see Appendix A)

The school teaches students how to cite sources correctly using appropriate referencing systems (such as Harvard or MLA). Students should ask their teachers if they are unsure how to cite a particular source.

Proper paraphrasing means:

• Reading and understanding the source material
• Putting ideas into your own words
• Restructuring sentences and paragraphs
• Still citing the original source

Simply changing a few words or rearranging the order of words is not proper paraphrasing and is considered plagiarism.

Seeking Help Appropriately

Students are encouraged to seek help with their learning, but they must ensure that submitted work is their own.

Appropriate help includes:

• Asking teachers for clarification or guidance
• Discussing ideas and concepts with classmates
• Receiving feedback on drafts (where permitted by the teacher)
• Using tutors or parents to explain concepts
• Using writing centres or study support services

Inappropriate help includes:

• Having someone else write or substantially rewrite your work
• Submitting work that has been heavily edited by others
• Having a tutor or parent complete assignments for you
• Purchasing or commissioning work from essay writing services

When in doubt, students should ask their teacher whether a particular form of help is appropriate.

Appendix A: Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools

Artificial Intelligence tools, such as ChatGPT, Google Bard, and other large language models, are increasingly available and can be useful learning aids. However, their use must be ethical and transparent.

Acceptable Use of AI Tools:

• Using AI to brainstorm ideas (if permitted by the teacher)
• Using AI to understand difficult concepts
• Using AI to check grammar and spelling
• Using AI as a research starting point

Students may ONLY use AI tools for assignments when:

• The teacher has explicitly given permission
• The use of AI is properly acknowledged in the work
• The student can explain and defend the ideas in their submission
• The AI output has been significantly modified and personalised

Unacceptable Use of AI Tools:

• Submitting AI-generated text as your own work without acknowledgment
• Using AI to write essays, reports, or assignments without permission
• Using AI during examinations unless explicitly permitted
• Over-relying on AI without developing your own understanding
• Using AI to complete work that is designed to assess your individual skills

How to Acknowledge AI Use:

If a teacher has permitted the use of AI tools, students must:

• Include a statement describing how AI was used
• Cite the specific AI tool and version used
• Include relevant prompts used
• Explain how the AI output was modified or incorporated

Example acknowledgment:
"I used ChatGPT (GPT-4, accessed 15 September 2023) to brainstorm initial ideas for this essay structure. I used the prompt: 'What are the main themes in Macbeth?' I then developed my own analysis and arguments independently."

Why Proper AI Use Matters:

• It develops critical thinking and your own writing skills
• It ensures you can explain and defend your work
• It maintains fairness in assessment
• It prepares you for examinations where AI will not be available
• It builds genuine knowledge and understanding

Consequences of Academic Misconduct

The school takes academic misconduct seriously. Consequences will depend on:

• The nature and severity of the misconduct
• Whether it was a first or repeated offence
• The student's age and understanding
• Whether the misconduct was intentional or unintentional

Possible consequences include:

First or Minor Offence:
• Educational conversation about academic integrity
• Requirement to resubmit work properly
• Zero mark for the assessment
• Loss of merit points
• Detention
• Letter home to parents

Serious or Repeated Offence:
• Zero mark for the assessment with no opportunity to resubmit
• Removal from examination entry
• Internal suspension
• In cases of examination misconduct, referral to the examination board
• In very serious cases, permanent exclusion

For external examinations (GCSE, A-Level), the school must report suspected malpractice to the examination board, which may result in disqualification from that subject or all subjects.

Prevention and Education

The school is committed to preventing academic misconduct through:

• Teaching students about academic integrity and proper citation
• Providing clear guidelines for each assignment
• Modelling good academic practice
• Using plagiarism detection software where appropriate
• Creating assessments that require original thinking and personalisation
• Fostering a culture of learning rather than just grades

Students' Responsibilities

Students are expected to:

• Understand and follow this Academic Integrity Policy
• Complete their own work honestly
• Cite sources properly
• Ask teachers if they are unsure about what is permitted
• Report concerns about academic misconduct
• Take responsibility for their own learning

Parents' Responsibilities

Parents are expected to:

• Support the school's Academic Integrity Policy
• Help students understand concepts without doing work for them
• Encourage honest effort and genuine learning
• Contact teachers if they have questions about what help is appropriate
• Model integrity and honesty

Staff Responsibilities

Staff are expected to:

• Teach students about academic integrity and proper citation
• Provide clear expectations for each assessment
• Design assessments that promote original work
• Investigate suspected cases of academic misconduct fairly
• Apply consequences consistently
• Report cases of academic misconduct to the relevant Head of Year

Further Information and Support

Students who need help with:

• Understanding academic integrity
• Citing sources correctly
• Paraphrasing and summarising
• Using AI tools appropriately
• Any other academic skills

Should speak to their subject teacher, form tutor, or the Learning Support Department.

Monitoring and Review

This policy will be reviewed annually by the Senior Leadership Team and the Board of Governors.
