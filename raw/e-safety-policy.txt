E-SAFETY POLICY
Haberdashers' Elstree Schools

Introduction

This E-Safety Policy applies to all members of the Haberdashers' Elstree Schools community (including staff, students, volunteers, parents/carers, visitors, and community users) who have access to and are users of Habs ICT systems, both in and out of school.

The school will deal with such incidents within this policy and associated behaviour and anti-bullying policies and will, where known, inform parents/carers of incidents of inappropriate e-safety behaviour that take place out of school.

Background

ICT in the 21st Century is seen as an essential resource to support learning and teaching, as well as playing an important role in the everyday lives of children, young people, and adults. Consequently, schools need to build in the use of these technologies in order to equip our young people with the skills to access life-long learning and employment.

Information and Communications Technology covers a wide range of resources including web-based and mobile learning. It is also important to recognise the constant and fast-paced evolution of ICT within our society as a whole. Currently the internet technologies children and young people are using both inside and outside of the classroom include:

• Websites
• Learning Platforms and Virtual Learning Environments
• Email and Instant Messaging
• Chat Rooms and Social Networking
• Blogs and Wikis
• Podcasting
• Video Broadcasting
• Music Downloading
• Gaming
• Mobile/Smart phones with text, video and/or web functionality
• Other mobile devices with web functionality

Whilst exciting and beneficial both in and out of the context of education, much ICT, particularly web-based resources, are not consistently policed. All users need to be aware of the range of risks associated with the use of these Internet technologies and that some have minimum age requirements (13 years for most social networking sites).

Roles and Responsibilities

The Headmaster and Senior Leadership Team

The Headmaster has overall responsibility for e-safety within the school. The Headmaster is responsible for ensuring that the E-Safety Coordinator and other relevant staff receive suitable training to enable them to carry out their e-safety roles.

The Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) is Mr James Smith, and the Deputy DSL is Mrs Sarah Johnson. They are responsible for coordinating safeguarding matters including e-safety.

E-Safety Coordinator

The E-Safety Coordinator is responsible for:
• Taking day to day responsibility for e-safety issues
• Ensuring that all staff are aware of the procedures that need to be followed in the event of an e-safety incident
• Providing training and advice for staff
• Liaising with the Local Authority and relevant agencies
• Receiving reports of e-safety incidents and creating a log of incidents

Teaching and Support Staff

All teaching and support staff are responsible for:
• Having an up to date awareness of e-safety matters
• Reading, understanding, and adhering to the e-safety policy
• Reporting any suspected misuse or problem to the E-Safety Coordinator
• Ensuring that students understand and follow the e-safety rules
• Embedding e-safety issues in all aspects of the curriculum

Students

Students are responsible for:
• Using school ICT systems in accordance with the Acceptable Use Policy
• Understanding the importance of reporting abuse, misuse, or access to inappropriate materials
• Knowing what to do if they or someone they know feels worried or vulnerable when using online technology

Mobile Phone Guidance - Appendix 1

Rationale

The school recognises that mobile phones are now an integral part of modern society. However, their use in school can be disruptive to the learning environment and can be used inappropriately.

Rules for Students:

1. Mobile phones must be switched off and kept out of sight during lessons unless explicit permission has been given by a member of staff.

2. Mobile phones may be used during break and lunch times in designated areas only.

3. Students must not use their mobile phones to take photographs, record videos or audio within the school premises without permission from a member of staff.

4. The sending of abusive, threatening, or inappropriate messages or images via mobile phone is strictly forbidden and will be treated as a serious breach of discipline.

5. Students who breach the mobile phone policy will have their phone confiscated and it will only be returned to a parent or carer.

6. The school accepts no responsibility for lost, stolen, or damaged mobile phones.

Staff Guidance on Mobile Phones:

Staff should not use their personal mobile phones during teaching time. Personal calls should be made during breaks or lunch time. In an emergency, staff should use the school office phone or receive calls via the school office.

Sanctions

Where students are found to have breached the E-Safety Policy or the Acceptable Use Policy, sanctions will be applied in line with the school's Behaviour Policy. These may include:

• Confiscation of device
• Withdrawal of ICT privileges
• Detention
• In serious cases, suspension or permanent exclusion

In cases where illegal activity is suspected, the school will contact the police.

Monitoring and Review

This policy will be reviewed annually by the Senior Leadership Team and the Board of Governors. The policy will be updated to reflect changes in technology and guidance from the Department for Education and local authorities.
